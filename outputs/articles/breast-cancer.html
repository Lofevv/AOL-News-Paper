School Life and Events

Breast Cancer Awareness Month

To mark Breast Cancer Awareness Month, the Academy of Leaders School featured personal testimonies and held a student-led charity bake sale.

In October the Academy of Leaders School held a number of activities to observe Breast Cancer Awareness Month, focusing on the importance of early detection, the value of having support and the strength that can be gained from community action. As part of these events, the school organized a charity bake sale and raised 10,300 LYD for the Cancer Centre in Sabratha.

As part of the initiative, AOLS had two speakers who gave accounts of their own deeply personal experiences with breast cancer and stressed the importance of recognising warning signs and getting medical help without delay. One speaker described a family member who initially ignored signs and put off getting medical advice. When she finally saw a doctor, she was diagnosed with breast cancer and underwent a mastectomy and further treatment. The story was a reminder to take changes in health seriously and seek medical advice.

The second speaker described her experience as a breast cancer survivor. When she first noticed a lump, it seemed insignificant and she did not think it could be serious. Medical examinations later found breast cancer. She had an operation and treatment, and recovered from the illness.

She stressed that changes should not be ignored and highlighted the importance of a strong support system, especially the encouragement and understanding provided by male family members and partners during diagnosis and treatment.

A few days later the school put its commitment to awareness into action by arranging a charity bake sale involving students and members of the school community. Students helped run the event, and many people provided homemade food, baked goods and other treats. Students and staff wore pink to show their support and solidarity with people affected by breast cancer.

The event showed how communities can make an impact when they unite and work together. Whether they helped prepare food, worked at the sale or donated, everyone contributed to the school’s effort.

In the end, AOLS collected 10,300 LYD for the Cancer Centre in Sabratha and turned awareness into practical action. More than the money raised, the month-long initiative emphasised early diagnosis, support and the value of donations. Through the speakers’ courage and the community’s generosity, AOLS showed the importance of standing with people affected by breast cancer.
