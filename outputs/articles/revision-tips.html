Last-Minute Revision Tips for IGCSE Students

Three students — Amal Shebani, Abdulsalam Dahan and Sama Ben Hamouda — share their experiences and revision advice for the final two weeks before IGCSE examinations.

With IGCSE examinations approaching, the final two weeks can feel like a race against time. Between unfinished revision, past papers and the pressure to remember everything, it can be difficult to know where to focus. Making the most of this time is not necessarily about studying longer, but about studying smarter.

1. Make past papers your priority

Students highlighted the value of practising past papers and becoming familiar with the structure and demands of examinations. Amal Shebani said she spent the final two weeks mainly on past papers, while also memorising essential content and reviewing earlier mistakes. She said she spent about five to six hours a day completing papers, with much of her revision focused on understanding her mistakes. Abdulsalam Dahan said about 75% of his revision time went to past papers. Their experiences point to the importance of applying knowledge, interpreting unfamiliar questions and managing time as well as learning content.

2. Understand mistakes rather than simply correcting them

Progress comes from understanding what went wrong. Abdulsalam said he changed the habit of leaving incorrect answers aside; instead, he recorded and reviewed them to avoid repeating the same mistakes. Sama Ben Hamouda described a similar approach: after covering the subject specifications and understanding the content, she used past papers to test her knowledge, find weaknesses and understand how to approach questions. Keeping a record of incorrect answers and revisiting difficult questions can make revision more effective.

3. Know when to stop revising and start practising

Amal and Abdulsalam said repeatedly reviewing familiar notes could become counterproductive, and that past papers and exam-style conditions were more beneficial. Sama took a different approach: she first reviewed the full curriculum for each subject, then moved on to past papers. Together, their experiences show that preparation needs balance. Understanding the syllabus matters, and so does practising and addressing weaknesses.

4. Avoid relying on predictions or leaving everything until the last minute

Sama advised students to review the full curriculum and, especially for English Language Paper 1, know all the texts they are expected to study. She warned against relying on predictions because exams may include questions and topics students did not anticipate. She also advised against leaving preparation until the day before an exam. Predictions cannot replace understanding the syllabus; preparation for a range of questions is more useful than memorising a limited set of predicted topics.

5. Make the most of classroom time

Revision does not begin two weeks before exams. Sama said one habit she deliberately stopped was not paying attention in class and not participating in classroom activities. Lessons offer a chance to clarify difficult concepts, ask questions and strengthen understanding before independent revision begins. Paying attention, participating and keeping up with material throughout the year can make the final revision period less overwhelming.

A final piece of advice

Although each student approached the final two weeks differently, their experiences shared several lessons: understand the syllabus, practise past papers, keep track of mistakes and focus on weaknesses. There is no single revision strategy for everyone. Some students may need more time to consolidate content, while others may benefit from examination practice.

The final two weeks should not be about trying to learn everything overnight. They should be about strengthening what you know, identifying what still needs work and going into the examination room feeling prepared. Study smart, learn from mistakes and aim to improve with every past paper.
