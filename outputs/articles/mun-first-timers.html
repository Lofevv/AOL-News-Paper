Beyond the Classroom

Tips for MUN First-Timers

After reading the beginner’s guide to MUN and becoming familiar with general procedure, use these notes to prepare for a first committee.

Rules of conduct and MUN etiquette

MUN is built around diplomacy, mutual respect and professional communication. Delegates can challenge one another’s arguments while maintaining a respectful environment. Every delegate is expected to:

  • Address the Chair appropriately.
  • Listen when another delegate is speaking.
  • Avoid interrupting or speaking without recognition.
  • Respect different political perspectives.
  • Remain professional during disagreements.
  • Follow the committee’s rules of procedure.
  • Participate actively and contribute constructively.

Delegates represent a country, not simply an individual opinion. Their conduct should reflect the professionalism expected in an international diplomatic setting.

Common MUN terms

Delegate
A participant representing an assigned country.
Dais
The team responsible for overseeing committee proceedings.
Placard
A sign displaying a country’s name, raised to request recognition.
Quorum
The minimum number of delegates required for a committee to conduct official business.
Bloc
A group of delegates working together because they share similar positions or interests.
Sponsor
A delegate or country that contributed significantly to a draft resolution and supports its introduction.
Signatory
A delegate who wants a draft resolution discussed. This does not necessarily mean they support it.
Yield
Giving up the remaining time after a speech, where the rules permit it.
Right of Reply
A procedural opportunity to respond to a direct insult to a country’s integrity or reputation, subject to the Chair’s discretion and conference rules.
Resolution
A formal document outlining proposed solutions to an issue.

Tips for first-time delegates

  • Prepare, but do not memorise everything. Understanding your country’s position is more useful than memorising an entire speech.
  • Do not be afraid to speak. Your first speech does not have to be perfect; confidence develops with practice.
  • Listen as much as you speak. Other delegates’ ideas can reveal opportunities to negotiate and build alliances.
  • Be diplomatic. Strong arguments matter, and so does respectful communication.
  • Get involved. Participate in caucuses, contribute to working papers and collaborate with other delegates.
  • Stay flexible. Negotiation can require compromise, so consider ideas that differ from your original proposal.

MUN is a learning experience. Mistakes are part of the process, and each committee session is an opportunity to improve.

Final thoughts

Model United Nations extends beyond the committee room. It encourages students to learn about international affairs while developing public speaking, critical thinking, research, leadership and negotiation skills.

At its core, MUN is about approaching complex issues, understanding perspectives different from your own and working with others towards meaningful solutions. You do not need to be the most experienced speaker in the room. What matters is a willingness to research, participate, listen and contribute.

Come prepared, stay curious and remember that every great delegate starts somewhere.
